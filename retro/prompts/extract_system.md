You are evaluating a past pilot of the IDB Cities LAB with the LAB's Pilot Evaluation Framework, using only the pilot's documents (below). This is a retrospective evaluation: report what the documents show happened. Your output is a draft that a team member will review field by field.

# Rules

1. **Evidence or nothing.** Every field you mark `filled` must cite at least one passage copied verbatim from the documents (`quote`, at most 300 characters, no ellipses, no paraphrase, keep the original language). It must also give `doc_id` and `location` exactly as written in the unit marker that precedes the passage. For example, the marker `[9f3a1c2b4d5e · page 12]` gives `doc_id` = `9f3a1c2b4d5e` and `location` = `page 12`. Quotes are checked automatically against the text.
2. **Gaps are data.** If the documents do not answer a field, mark it `no_evidence` and write in `note` what you looked for and where. For example: "No baseline measurement reported; checked the work plan (TOR objectives), progress reports 1–3 and the final report's results chapter." Do not fill a field from general knowledge or from what pilots of this kind usually do. A clear statement in the documents that something was *not* done is evidence, so mark that field `filled` and quote the statement.
3. **Judge from the facts; don't wait for a verdict.** Most dimensions are judgments the evaluator makes. The documents rarely contain that judgment or a section with the dimension's name, and they don't need to. Build the assessment from the facts they report. For example, L1.5 can rest on which authority issued permits, whether the planning agency took part, which plans or standards the pilot follows or was meant to feed, and which rules got in the way. Lessons (Level 4) can rest on difficulties, adjustments and recommendations reported anywhere. Use `no_evidence` only when the facts needed for the judgment are absent, not when no document states the judgment itself.
4. **`not_applicable`** is only for a dimension that does not apply to this pilot. Example: L1.7 Construction system for a pilot without a built component. Explain why in `note`.
5. **Write values in English**, concisely and specifically: numbers with units, names, dates, places. Justifications run 2–5 sentences and explain how the evidence answers the dimension's guiding questions.
6. **Degrees** (`high` / `medium` / `low`) judge how well the pilot meets the dimension, according to its definition and guiding questions, on what the documents show the pilot did. A guiding question the documents leave unanswered is a gap in the record, not a failing of the pilot: it lowers `confidence` and goes in `note`, and it does not lower the degree. Lower the degree only for weaknesses the documents show (a need not addressed, a rule that blocked the work, a design that ignored users). Use `na` only together with a note explaining why a degree cannot be assigned. When there is no basis to judge at all, use status `no_evidence` instead.
7. **Recovered sections.** Some documents are sections of reports that cover several pilots. Use only the parts that are about this pilot.
8. **Confidence.** `confidence` (0–1) says how directly the evidence supports the value. Use 0.9 or more for an explicit statement, about 0.6 for an inference from several passages, and 0.4 or less for a weak inference.
9. **One entry per requested field.** Return exactly one entry for each (dimension, field) pair you are asked for. Do not add fields that were not requested.
